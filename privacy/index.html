How I handle your information

For everyone who asks about, applies for or lives in one of my rental houses: what I collect, why, who helps me process it, and how long I keep it. Effective November 8, 2026.

The short version

  • I collect what I need to show a house, screen applications fairly and run a tenancy. Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.
  • Screening happens inside Zillow's application. I never ask for a Social Security number anywhere else, and I never store one.
  • An ID check through Stripe protects showings of a vacant house. The selfie part is optional; a short FaceTime works too.
  • Email rentals@scholl.email for a copy, a correction or deletion. I answer within 30 days.

Who I am

I'm Constantin Scholl. I own a few rental houses in the Austin area and manage them myself. You can reach me at rentals@scholl.email or +1 (512) 688-8229.

What I collect

Contact details
Your name, phone number and email, from the listing site that connected us or from you directly.
What you tell me
Your messages, calls and voicemails on my business number, including transcripts of voicemails, and what you say about move-in dates, occupants, pets, income and rental history while we talk about a house.
Applications
If you apply, Zillow Applications collects the application and runs the credit and background reports through its providers, under Zillow's own terms. I see the application and those reports and keep only what my written criteria need.
ID verification
Before a showing of a vacant house, an ID check through Stripe Identity: a photo of a government ID and, unless you'd rather not, a selfie that Stripe compares with it.
The house
Photos and videos of the house, move-in and move-out condition records, and the repair history.
Payments
Deposits, rent and refunds, in the records of the payment service we use.

Why

Showings and their safety; screening every application against the same written criteria; the lease; repairs, deposits and the deposit accounting; and the records landlord-tenant, fair-housing and tax law require. My written criteria come with every listing and application, and I apply them the same way to everyone, as the Fair Housing Act requires.

Who helps me

These companies process information on my behalf, each under its own privacy and security terms:

  • Google Workspace: email, calendar and documents.
  • Zillow, Apartments.com and Avail: listings and messages (Avail also shows my listings on its partner sites); applications go through Zillow.
  • Telnyx: texts, calls and voicemail on my business number.
  • Slack: my own notifications about messages and tasks.
  • Dropbox Sign: signing leases and renewals: your name, email and signature.
  • Cloudflare: the private archive of texts and voicemails I read on my phone.
  • GitHub: the private records of my management system.
  • Anthropic: an AI assistant that helps me draft and keep track of messages; I read every message and make every decision myself.

None of it is sold. Stripe runs the ID check for showings, but not on my behalf: Stripe and I are independent controllers. Stripe decides what it does with your ID and selfie and answers for that, and I do the same for the result it sends me. Stripe's own privacy policy (stripe.com/privacy) covers Stripe's part; this page covers mine.

The ID check

Showing a vacant house means letting someone I've never met inside, so I verify who I'm meeting. Stripe reads the ID and, if you choose the selfie, compares the face in it with the ID photo. That comparison is a measurement of facial features; Stripe performs it, I never receive or store it, and I see only "verified" and whether the name matches. I have Stripe delete the images and data after the showing, or once we stop talking, and within a year at the latest. You can always skip the selfie: send the ID photo only and we'll do a short FaceTime at the door. The ID check never counts in an application decision.

Social Security numbers

I never ask for a Social Security number by text, email, phone or form. Zillow's application asks for it to run the reports; it stays in Zillow's system, and I don't copy it anywhere.

How long I keep it

These are the longest I mean to keep each kind of record. I'm still setting up the deletion that carries out this schedule, so until it runs some records may stay longer; if you want yours deleted sooner, just ask.

Email stays as the written record of a tenancy; tax records follow the tax rules.

When a record is no longer kept, I delete it from my records and from the services above that hold a copy, and it leaves the encrypted backup as older backups expire. Paper with personal information is shredded.

How it's protected

My records live on an encrypted laptop in a private system only I can open, behind two-factor sign-in, with an encrypted backup. Documents with an ID, a report or a pay stub go in a private folder. If a breach ever affected you, I would tell you as Texas law requires.

Reply STOP to any text to stop texts from my business number.
